Bar does not fit. The rigid central bar is longer than the gap it has to span, so it must bow and the cage must strain. This is every θ except ±θ*, and it is why only two states exist.

What the animation is showing

Each hub turns about its own outward axis and nothing else — the six vertex coordinates and the six axis directions never change, which is the whole point: the LHD fusion sites stay exactly where a lattice needs them. Because the hub is rigid, the two arms facing each other across it sweep in opposite directions, so the net force on the hub is zero and only torque comes out. That torque is the signal a neighbouring cage would feel.

The central bar is built at one fixed length. Sweep θ by hand and watch the required span: it is smallest at θ = 0 and grows with |θ| in either direction. So a bar cut to fit at +θ* also fits at −θ*, and fits nowhere else. The untwisted cage is not a high-energy intermediate — the bar is simply too long to assemble it. Two states, from rigid-link geometry alone.
