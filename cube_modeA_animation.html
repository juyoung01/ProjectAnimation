Mode A on the cube — one hinge per edge, animated

Eight vertices pinned in space. Each edge is one arm rigidly fused to its hub, a single hinge, and a second arm into the neighbouring hub — half the hinges of Mode B. The only freedom is that every hub turns in place, and because the cube graph is bipartite the two colours turn in opposite senses. The arms do not meet. Each pair of half-edges is built at a fixed length ℓ, so at this twist their ends miss each other by the misfit above — they would have to interpenetrate (θ small) or fall short. This is every θ except ±θ*, and it is why only two states exist.

What the animation is showing

Every hub turns about its own outward body-diagonal axis and does nothing else — the eight vertex coordinates and the eight axis directions never change. That is the whole point: the fusion sites stay exactly where a lattice needs them, and the in-place rotation is the output signal.

Put every hub at twist σθ, where σ = s₁s₂s₃ is the product of the vertex's coordinate signs — the bipartite 2-colouring of the cube. Then for every edge the two arm ends agree in two of their three components identically, for every θ. Only the component along the edge is left, and it gives one scalar equation.

That equation solves in closed form:

ℓ_req(θ) = 3 / (2 cos θ + 1)

ℓ_req is exactly even in θ and strictly increasing in |θ|. So an arm cut to one length fits at exactly two twists, +θ* and −θ*, and at nothing in between — the two states are mirror images, and the untwisted cube is not a high-energy intermediate but geometrically unassemblable. Inverting gives the design point directly:

cos θ* = ½ (3/ℓ − 1)

Why the cube and not the octahedron. One hinge per edge forces adjacent hubs to turn in opposite senses, which requires the vertex graph to be 2-colourable. The octahedron's graph is K2,2,2 — it carries 8 triangular faces, so no 2-colouring exists and Mode A is impossible there for any buckling direction. The cube's graph has no odd cycle, so the obstruction simply is not present. Solving the rigid-hub conditions numerically agrees: the octahedron never gets below a residual of 0.21, the cube reaches 2·10⁻¹⁶.
What this animation is and is not. This is the idealised rigid-link kinematics, exactly as modeB_animation.html is for Mode B — hubs are rigid bodies, arms are rigid, the hinge is an ideal revolute joint. It shows that the cube admits the Mode B behaviour with one hinge per edge, that the states are discrete (0 internal DOF), and that the shape change is small. It does not establish a protein design: the hinge's real screw axis and its +2.142 Å translation have not been checked against the required joint, no parts have been placed, and nothing here is energy-minimised, sequence-designed or folding-validated. Treat the numbers as a target to build toward, not as a result.
