Model A in 3D — a 3×3×3 block of even-prism cages

The unit is an even prism with one hinge per edge, the only family that survives a strict count. Every even prism has three independent antipodal corner axes, so the cages stack as a 3D block with six neighbours each. Press play and the whole block switches together; the welded corners stay welded the entire way.

What the block shows

A bond translates the neighbour along a corner axis, so a 3D block needs three independent antipodal axes. Every even prism has them. Colouring sites by (i+j+k) parity 2-colours the bond graph, so every cycle is even and the block can never be frustrated whatever J turns out to be — measured: 27 cages, 54 bonds, one cage forces all 27, exactly two global states.

prism 4 → J = +1, uniform, 109.47° ×3  ·  prism 6 → J = −1, checkerboard, 101.54° ×3  ·  prism 8 → J = +1, uniform, 82.66/82.66/138.12°

The corners stay welded through the whole sweep. Drag s away from ±1 and the fusion mismatch stays at 10⁻¹⁷ — the welded corners turn together from end to end, which is the propagation. What cannot follow is each cage's own closure: its arms are cut for θ*, so they open by up to 0.14 in between (shown as amber dashes). That is why only s = ±1 are real states, and why the switch is all-or-none rather than a continuous motion.

Press Randomise the state to put cages out of step: roughly half the 54 fusions break and the mismatch jumps to 0.75. A cage cannot sit out of step with its neighbours.

Scope. Pure rigid-body geometry — no energies, forces, compliance, proteins or dynamics. Steric overlap between non-bonded cages is not checked. The mid-sweep frames are not physical states.
